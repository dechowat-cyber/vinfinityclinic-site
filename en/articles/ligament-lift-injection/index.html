Myth vs Evidence

Do "ligament lift"
injections really work?

You may have heard that a sagging face comes from loose ligaments, and that filler, PN or collagen stimulators can tighten them. New research changes that picture. Here is a simple summary.

By Dechowat Promda, M.D. · Updated 10 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short What facial ligaments are What the new research says Injecting to tighten ligaments What still makes sense What each product does Questions to ask your doctor References

In short

  • A 2023 study found that facial ligaments do not loosen with age; they become stiffer.1
  • Injecting to "tighten ligaments" therefore has a weaker rationale, and human evidence is very limited.2
  • What still makes sense is restoring deep support, using the ligament lines as landmarks.3
  • Each product works in a different layer, so the choice should match the layer that has actually changed.

What facial ligaments are

Picture a curtain hung on hooks. Facial ligaments are the hooks that pin skin and soft tissue to bone. The fabric hanging between the hooks is your fat and skin. With age, the fabric sags into folds and jowls.

What the new research says

It was long believed that the hooks (the ligaments) stretched and loosened. An experimental study published in Aesthetic Surgery Journal in 2023, with Prof. Sebastian Cotofana, found the ligaments did not loosen but became stiffer, with thicker collagen bundles.1 What really sags is the fabric between the hooks. Read the details in Do facial ligaments really loosen with age?

Worth knowing: the study was done in rats and focused mainly on the cheekbone ligament. Some ligaments, such as those around the eyes, may change differently.

Does injecting to "tighten ligaments" still work?

If the hooks are already firm, injecting into them to make them firmer does not fix the sagging fabric.

  • Human evidence for injecting to thicken a ligament is currently a single case report based on ultrasound.2
  • No study has yet clearly measured a long-term lifting effect from injecting the ligament itself.

What still makes sense: restoring support

As bone and deep fat gradually recede with age, the fabric between the hooks loses its support. Rebuilding support in the deep layer, near where the ligaments attach to bone, helps carry the tissue above. This principle does not depend on ligaments being loose, so it still fits the new data.34

Put simply: don't pull the hooks; give the fabric something to rest on.

Questions to ask your doctor before treatment

  1. Which layer is causing my sagging? Bone, deep fat or skin?
  2. Which layer will you inject, and why? If the answer is "to lift the ligament", ask what evidence supports it.
  3. Are there major blood vessels near this area? How will you protect against complications?5
