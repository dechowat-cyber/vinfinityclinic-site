Where to get dermal filler in Udon Thani:
7 things to check before choosing a clinic

Whichever clinic you choose in Udon Thani, this checklist helps you make sure you get a real doctor, a genuine product and a licensed medical facility. You can verify every point yourself through official sources.

At a glance

  • The clinic must hold a clinic (medical facility) license, and the injector must be a doctor with a license to practise medicine. Both can be checked online.
  • The filler must be registered with the Thai FDA, and the box should be opened in front of you before the injection.
  • A good clinic assesses you before quoting a price, tells you the risks plainly and has a plan for managing complications.

A 7-point checklist for choosing a filler clinic in Udon Thani

  1. Clinic license The clinic must be licensed to operate as a medical facility. You can check this using the private medical facility search of the Department of Health Service Support (HSS)1.
  2. A doctor does the injecting Ask for the name of the doctor who will inject you, then check it against the Medical Council of Thailand's register2. Filler injection is a medical procedure, and only doctors may perform it.
  3. Thai FDA-registered product Ask to see the box, the registration number and the expiry date before the injection, then check the registration number in the Thai FDA's system3.
  4. Assessment before a price The doctor should look at your facial structure, explain where filler is needed and why, and tell you the full cost before starting, rather than selling you a number of cc.
  5. Honest about risks This includes swelling, bruising, lumps and vascular occlusion, which is rare but serious. You should be asked to sign a consent form before treatment.
  6. A plan for complications The clinic should keep the filler-dissolving enzyme (hyaluronidase) and an emergency protocol ready to use. Read more in our article on filler safety.
  7. Follow-up appointments There should be a follow-up visit to review your results, and you should be able to reach the doctor if anything seems wrong.

Questions to ask your doctor before an injection

  • What is causing the hollow or area that concerns me, and is filler the right option for it?
  • Which product will you use, how much, and in which layer?
  • Will you use a needle or a cannula, and why?
  • If a complication occurs, what will the clinic do, and how can I contact the doctor?
  • What does the total cost include, and when do I need to come back?

Warning signs

  • An unusually low price, or a refusal to open the box in front of you
  • No doctor on site, or no name given for the injecting doctor
  • Promises of a specific result, or claims that there is no risk at all
  • Injections given outside a medical facility, such as at home, in a hotel or in a beauty salon
