Summary

  • Hyaluronic acid (HA) filler can be dissolved with the enzyme hyaluronidase, assessed and injected by a doctor.
  • Some non-HA fillers cannot be dissolved with enzyme, so tell the doctor what you had injected, where, and when.
  • If you have severe pain, skin that turns pale or develops a dark patch, or any vision problems after injection, see a doctor immediately.

Common filler problems

  • A lump you can feel or see Usually caused by injecting too superficially, or using a gel that doesn't suit the area.
  • A blue-grey tint under the eyes (Tyndall effect) Caused by gel sitting too close to the surface under thin skin.
  • A face that looks too full or puffy Especially when smiling, from too much volume or injection in the wrong layer.
  • Asymmetry, or gel that has moved out of place

Read why the gel must be matched to the right layer in our article on the 5 layers of facial anatomy.

How we correct it at Vinfinity Clinic

  1. History What was injected, how much, when, and whether you have any drug allergies.
  2. Assessment The doctor checks where the gel is and which layer it sits in, and distinguishes filler from fluid swelling or your own tissue.
  3. Planning Only the areas that need it are dissolved; there is no need to dissolve everything.
  4. Follow-up Once everything has settled, the doctor considers whether new filler should be placed in the correct layer.

Warning signs that need a doctor straight away

  • Pain that keeps getting worse after injection
  • Skin that turns pale white, develops a dusky net-like pattern, or darkens in a strip along a blood vessel
  • Blurred vision, abnormal vision or eye pain

These symptoms may signal a blocked blood vessel (vascular occlusion), which is rare but needs treatment as quickly as possible. Read more in our article on dermal filler safety.
